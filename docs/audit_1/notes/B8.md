# B8: one scheduler for the whole run

A design note for the user's decision (audit 1, P5 S3; [04 B8](../04_Bold_Proposals.md#b8--one-scheduler-for-the-whole-run)).
It gives the mechanics, a simulated schedule on the real zeus plans, the options, and a recommendation.
Nothing here is built.

---

## 1. Today

Execution is three nested mechanisms:
1. **Groups within a point**, run in order; a group's tools start together (a FIFO chain must: L8), are
   supervised as one, and settle (count checks, `.partial` → final) before the next group starts.
2. **Points within a run**: `parallelism` at a time, in point order, on a thread pool
   (`execute.run_points`); the user picks `parallelism` against the core count, and `crowded()` warns.
3. **Stages and runs**: `pre` → points → combined groups → `post` → plots, in `cli.run_one`; under
   `sweep_runs`, one run after another.

So a slot that frees up near the end of a run stays idle until the run's last point ends, and a
combined group waits for every point of its run, not only its own.

---

## 2. Mechanics of one scheduler

- **Nodes.** Each *group* of each point and stage is a node, never a single tool: a group's tools share
  FIFOs, so they must start together and be supervised as one (L8: a FIFO blocks until both ends open).
  `run_group` stays exactly as it is (the stop ladder, V32; the attribution rule: the first nonzero
  exit not a SIGPIPE), so failure handling carries over unchanged.
- **Edges.** A point's groups in order (files between them); a combined group after its own points; post
  after every point; a pre stage before every point; the plots after their configuration's points.
  Across a sweep of runs, nothing joins two configurations, so their nodes interleave freely.
- **The core budget.** Each node costs `execute.cores(plan)` (L6: a generator's threads, Rivet's
  shards, a table's `cores`). The scheduler starts any ready node, in plan order, while the cores in use
  plus its own stay within `[run] cores = "all" | N`; a node bigger than the budget runs alone.
  `parallelism` becomes a cap on points at once (or goes).
- **Stops and failures.** A stop (Ctrl-C) starts no more nodes and stops the running groups through their
  ladders, as today; a failed node fails its point, whose later nodes are skipped; other points go on.
- **What goes:** `run_points`' pool, the stage branching in `run_one` and `post.run_*` (~150 lines), for
  a scheduler of ~120. The event stream (V72) already makes the views indifferent to the order.

## 3. Simulated schedule (2026-10-03)

The real plans of `configs/PhotoProduction/zeus_validation.toml` (`sweep_runs`, 6 runs × 4 points,
`parallelism = 2`, 12 cores a point, 24 cores), each point costing its events at the measured
4,425 events/s (the 10M-event points took 2,260 s):

| Scenario | Today | One DAG | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| zeus as configured | 67.2 h | 67.2 h | **0 %** |
| `nompi` points 40 % cheaper (MPI off) | 67.2 h | 67.2 h | 0 % |
| one PDF 30 % dearer than the others | 77.2 h | 73.3 h | 5 % |
| 3 points a run instead of 4 (2 at once) | 67.2 h | 54.0 h | 20 % |

The zeus sweep is the scheduler's best case today: every run is four equal points, two at a time, so
no slot ever waits. B8 pays only where runs are uneven: a point count that does not divide by
`parallelism`, points of very different cost, combined groups or a post stage (the eic replicas),
or a mix of small and large points that could fill each other's gaps. The measured "after" on a zeus
sweep would therefore show nothing; the simulation is the evidence: `python3 docs/audit_1/notes/B8_sim.py
PhotoProduction/zeus_validation` (it plans, reads nothing else, and runs nothing).

## 4. Options

| | What | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Build the DAG scheduler (§2) | every uneven sweep gains (5–20 % above); combined and post start as early as they can; `cores` replaces the `parallelism` arithmetic | the highest risk of the audit, for no gain on the sweep the user runs most |
| **B (recommended)** | Keep the structure; add the two cheap parts: **`parallelism = "auto"`** (F10: `cores // max(cores per point)`), and **a pipelined sweep of runs**: under `sweep_runs`, the next run's points may start in the current run's free slots (its plan is made up front already, V54), the stages of each run still in order | the 3-points-a-run case's 20 % at a small part of the risk; nothing changes inside a run | combined groups still wait for their run's points |
| **C** | Nothing | no risk | the uneven cases keep their idle slots |

**Recommendation: B.** The zeus sweep, the one that runs for days, gains nothing from a full scheduler;
the cases that do gain mostly come from idle slots at the end of a run, which the pipelined sweep
fills without touching groups, attribution or the stop ladder.

**To decide:** A, B or C; and for B, whether a pipelined sweep is the default under `sweep_runs`, or a
key (`[run] pipeline = true`).
